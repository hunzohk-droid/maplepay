Gross to net calculator BC: what changes your pay?

Gross pay is your employment income before deductions. Net pay, also called take-home pay, is what remains after deductions.

MaplePay estimates net pay by subtracting federal income tax, BC income tax, CPP (including CPP2 where applicable) and EI from gross pay. Credits reduce the income tax estimate; enhanced CPP contributions reduce taxable income. Benefits, pension contributions and other deductions on your payslip can change what reaches your bank account.

This BC pay calculator estimates regular employment income. It does not calculate sales tax, business tax or income tax for other provinces.

How we calculate

We deduct enhanced CPP contributions from employment income before applying the tax brackets. We then apply basic personal credits, base CPP and EI credits, the federal Canada employment amount and the BC tax reduction, where eligible.

Frequently asked questions

How much tax is deducted from my paycheque in BC?

It depends on your income. In 2026, BC tax starts at 5.6% up to $50,363, plus federal tax from 14%, plus CPP (5.95%) and EI (1.63%). Enter your pay above for an estimated breakdown.

What is the BC basic personal amount for 2026?

$13,216 — the first $13,216 you earn is free from BC tax, worth a $740.10 credit. The federal basic personal amount is up to $16,452 and is reduced at higher net incomes.

What is CPP2?

An extra 4% CPP on earnings between $74,600 and $85,000. It applies when your pensionable earnings exceed the first CPP earnings ceiling.

Why is my actual paycheque different from this estimate?

Your employer uses your TD1 claims (spouse, dependants and other credits). MaplePay assumes only the basic personal claims. Actual deductions can also differ because of payroll rounding, year-to-date contributions, taxable benefits, pension contributions and other adjustments. This calculator is a simplified annual estimate, not an exact payroll calculation.

How to use this BC salary calculator

Enter your gross pay in Canadian dollars before deductions. Choose hourly, weekly, bi-weekly, monthly or annual pay, then select how often you receive a paycheque. For hourly pay, enter your usual hours per week.

Bi-weekly pay means 26 payments per year. Semi-monthly pay means 24 payments per year. The annual estimate is divided by your selected number of payments; actual payroll deductions may vary throughout the year.

About MaplePay and this estimate

MaplePay is a free calculator for estimating employment take-home pay in British Columbia. It is an independent tool and is not affiliated with the Canada Revenue Agency or the Government of British Columbia.

The full-year calculation uses the displayed annual tax brackets and employment credits, then subtracts income tax, CPP and EI. It includes base CPP and EI tax credits, enhanced CPP deductions, the federal Canada employment amount, the federal basic personal amount phase-out and the BC tax reduction. It excludes RRSP/RPP contributions, union dues, dependants, tuition, disability and age credits, bonuses, multiple jobs and refundable benefits. It is not a tax-return or payroll-withholding engine.

2026 payroll timing: The full-year BC lowest tax rate is 5.60%. CRA uses a prorated 6.14% withholding rate for July–December 2026 to catch up for the lower rate used earlier in the year. MaplePay uses the full-year rate and $690 annual tax reduction; it does not reproduce this mid-year payroll adjustment.
